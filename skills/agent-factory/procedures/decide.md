# Decide

Use this subskill for a consequential product, architecture or strategy choice
with more than one credible path, especially one costly to reverse. Routine,
reversible implementation choices belong to whoever does the work.

## Method

1. **Frame it:** the decision, what is at stake, the hard constraints, the
   unknowns, and how reversible each outcome is. Bad framing is the most common
   cause of bad decisions, so get it right first.
2. **Gather evidence** from the code, docs, decisions, external sources and user
   constraints, preferring evidence to confident assertion.
3. **Lay out two or three credible options,** each with its assumptions,
   benefits, costs, risks and the moving parts it adds.
4. **Recommend** the simplest option that meets every hard constraint, and say
   what it trades away and what uncertainty remains.

For a high-stakes or contested choice, have separate agents argue for and
against the strongest options and another judge, so no agent both advocates and
judges one option. Without subagents, do the passes separately and say
independence was limited. Skip this where it would not change your confidence.

A choice that changes strategy needs the authority the core rules require;
without it, return a recommendation, not a decision. Record the decision per the
[record test](../references/atelier.md#records), and have the strategy and docs
link to it instead of restating its rationale.

## Handoff

Also report the framing, the options and why each was rejected, the strongest
dissent, the authority for the decision and where it is recorded. Do not
implement the choice under this subskill.
